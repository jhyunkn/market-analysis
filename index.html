    How this is built

    23 FRED series, each judged against a fixed threshold written in advance (for example, core PCE above 3% counts as bad for stocks; a Baa corporate spread under 1.8 points counts as good). The regime chart groups every month since 1950 by its condition, using data already published that month, and shows the S&P 500's median return over the next year, measured against the median of all months (a normal year). Bars close to the dashed line mean the condition told you little. Hover a bar for the 1-in-10 bad year and sample size.

    How to read

    Percentile is the share of all past days with a lower reading; the bar runs from calm (left) to extreme stress (right). "Past days like today" are days whose reading sat within 5 percentile points of today's, and the S&P columns are its price change over the next 3 and 12 months after those days. Episodes count separate stretches (a gap of more than a month starts a new one): that is the real sample size. Medians are taken over days, so long sell-offs count more than short ones, which tends to make stressed setups look rosier than counting each episode once; fewer than 12 episodes is flagged as a small sample. Each gauge is read alone, gauges overlap heavily, and none of this has been tested as a timing rule. S&P price only, no dividends.

    How to read, and the limits

    Insider trades come from SEC Form 4 filings (filed within two business days). Open-market buys are the meaningful signal: research finds stocks bought by insiders, especially by several insiders at once, have tended to beat the market modestly over the following months. Sales are weaker evidence because insiders sell for many reasons, and pre-planned 10b5-1 sales are scheduled in advance, so they are shown separately; grants and option exercises are left out. Dollar values use today's price. Institutional changes come from 13F filings: positions as of the quarter end, filed up to 45 days later, long stock only (no shorts, and options are not netted), so they can be 2 to 4 months old. Hedge funds tracked: Berkshire, Bridgewater, Renaissance, Citadel, Millennium, Point72, Two Sigma, D. E. Shaw, Tiger Global, Coatue, Pershing Square, Viking, Lone Pine, Duquesne, Appaloosa, Third Point, Elliott, Soros, Scion, Baupost, Balyasny, Marshall Wace, Altimeter, Maverick, ARK, Tudor, Dragoneer, D1, Whale Rock, Greenlight and Icahn, found among each stock's 400 largest holders; Jane Street and Susquehanna are shown per stock as trading firms because their filings mostly reflect hedging. Short interest is reported by FINRA twice a month. Click any bar to open that stock. Analysis, not investment advice.

    Left of "Today" is the last six months of closing prices with the 50- and 200-day averages.     What this is based on

    Every number on this tab comes from five inputs. None of them is a forecast; each has a known weakness, listed under it.

    • PricesDaily closes from Yahoo Finance since 2017, adjusted for splits and dividends, through the latest completed market close.This window is one long bull market with a few sharp breaks, so "normal" here reflects a generally favorable era.
    • Likely and possible rangesEach stock's own volatility: today's level (exponentially weighted, recent days count most) fading toward its 1-year average with a 3-month half-life. Bands are 2-in-3 and 9-in-10 outcomes with no up or down drift.Real moves have fatter tails than these bands; a crash can land outside the light band.
    • If the S&P repeats historyThe median S&P 500 path over the year after past sell-offs like today (VIX 30 or higher with the S&P 8–20% below its high, since 1990, each episode counted once), scaled by the stock's trailing five-year beta to SPY. Newer listings use all available post-IPO history. Leveraged funds instead compound the underlying's path day by day: price x (underlying path)^3 x exp(-3 x the underlying's cumulative variance) x financing and fees (2 x the 3-month T-bill plus 0.95% a year).A median across few episodes; any single year can be far from it, and beta drifts.
    • Setups like this, for each stockPast days after the first one-year warm-up when the stock sat within 5 points of today's distance below its 52-week high and on the same side of its 200-day average. Matched days bunch together, so each episode (matches less than a month apart) counts once, from its first day; episodes whose 12-month windows overlap are merged into one cluster and averaged. The rate is the share of clusters higher 12 months later, against non-overlapping 12-month windows on the same history. Fewer than 8 independent clusters shows "insufficient sample" and is not scored.With prices since 2017, few stocks reach 8 clusters, so this check is usually not scored. Survivorship: these are stocks that are big today.
    • Analyst targetsConsensus from Nasdaq's public analyst data, refreshed with every data build: buy/hold/sell counts and the low, average and high 12-month target, with the date it last changed when available. A stock whose targets have not refreshed for 45 days is marked stale and not scored.Targets lag prices; many were set before this sell-off and tend to be optimistic.
    • Action state and levelsFour checks score the stock: trend (price vs 200-day, 50-day slope), setups-like-this edge of 10+ points over its normal year, the S&P history path lifting it 5%+, and a reachable analyst target with 70%+ buy ratings. "Confirms above" is the nearer moving average above the price (or the 1-month high when above both). "Nearest support" is the main bottom zone on the bottom map. Leveraged funds (TQQQ on QQQ, UPRO on SPY, TECL on XLK, SOXL on SOXX; see config/leveraged.json): zones and the confirmation level are built on the underlying and translated as fund price x (1 + 3 x the underlying's move), a one-session estimate recalculated daily; their "Avoid for now" rests on conditions visible today (underlying downtrend, volatility 1.4x normal or more, unsettled credit) and names the ones that fired; crash replay covers only the fund's own life, and a separate simulated stress test covers 2000 and 2008.Rules written in advance. Walk-forward tests show no edge so far: the trend and setups-like-this checks did not separate 3-month winners from losers (see the track record above the ranking), and the bottom zones held no more often than random levels (see the bottom map). Read action states as descriptions of the current setup. Not advice.
    • Macro regime23 FRED series judged against fixed thresholds, and the S&P 500's next-year returns after past months with each condition since 1950, using only data published by then.Across every condition, median next-year returns stayed within a few points of normal: macro has been a weak timing signal.
    • Momentum and breadthMomentum is the 12-month return skipping the last month, against this month's return relative to the S&P. Breadth is the share of the covered stocks above their 50- and 200-day averages.About 125 large US stocks, not the whole market; survivorship applies.
